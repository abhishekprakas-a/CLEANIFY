01 — Overview

What Cleanify is

Cleanify is a management system for a service-based cleaning company — the kind that sends crews out to clean water tanks, bathrooms, interlocked driveways, solar panels and vehicles. It replaces the usual mix of phone notebooks, WhatsApp photos and spreadsheets with a single system of record that connects the office and the field.

The office side is a web console where staff take bookings, schedule and assign crews, and watch performance. The field side is an installable phone app that gives each technician their day's jobs, turn-by-turn context, and a guided checklist that captures photographic proof of the work. Everything a technician does flows straight back to the office in real time.

Office

Bookings, scheduling, crew assignment, customer records, reviews, attendance and reporting — all in one dashboard.

Field

A phone-installable app with each technician's jobs, a step-by-step work flow, and before/after photo capture.

Proof

Every job carries timestamped before/after photos, a full status history, and a customer rating — auditable end to end.

02 — People

Who uses it

Access is role-based. Every screen and every API call is gated by the signed-in user's role, enforced both at the network edge and again in the server, so the two audiences only ever see what belongs to them.

Admin / office staff full console

Runs the business: manages customers and bookings, schedules and assigns crews, verifies new technician accounts, reviews job photos, tracks attendance, and reads the reports. Calling staff who take bookings sit in this role.

Technician field app

Sees only their assigned jobs. Checks in for the day, travels to site, captures before photos, does the work, captures after photos, and completes the job — all from their phone, even on a shaky connection.

04 — Lifecycle

The job lifecycle

Every job moves through a controlled sequence of states. The system enforces the allowed transitions and records who moved it, when, and why — so a job can never skip a step, and its whole history is always reconstructable.

1 Booking → Job

Office takes a booking (or a one-step "job card" that creates the customer, booking and job together), sets service, work name, line items and total.

2 Schedule & assign

The job is put on a date/slot and assigned to one or several technicians, with a conflict check so no one is double-booked.

3 Execute & complete

The technician reaches site, captures before photos, cleans, captures after photos, and completes — proof attached, no back-and-forth.

05 — In the field

What the technician does

The field app is a guided checklist — it only ever shows the one action that's valid right now, so there's nothing to get wrong. A technician can't start a job without checking in for the day, can't start cleaning without a before photo, and can't complete without an after photo.

  • Check in for the day (attendance is recorded automatically).
  • Open the job — customer, address with a one-tap Maps link, and the exact line items to clean with their risk ratings.
  • Before photos → Start cleaning. Photos upload straight to secure cloud storage; the job advances the moment they're in.
  • After photos → Complete job. The job closes with its proof attached — no waiting on an approver.
  • Manage photos — retake or delete a shot before finishing; oversized images are compressed on the phone and capped at 5 MB.
Design choice — speed over ceremony

The workflow was deliberately streamlined so crews aren't blocked waiting for an office approval mid-job. Photos are still captured as mandatory proof and are reviewable by the office at any time — the office keeps oversight without slowing the field down.

06 — Access control

Onboarding & verification

New technicians can self-register from a public sign-up page — but an account stays pending and cannot sign in until an admin verifies it. This keeps the workforce list clean and stops anyone unknown from reaching the system.

1 · Sign up

A prospective technician submits their details and a password. The account is created as pending.

2 · Verify

An admin sees the request in Account approvals and either approves it (activates sign-in) or rejects it (deactivates, reversibly).

3 · Work

Once approved, the technician signs in and starts receiving assigned jobs. Self-signup is technician-only — admins are created internally.

09 — Engineering

How it's built

Cleanify is one modern TypeScript codebase that serves both the web console and the field app. It's a Next.js 14 application: the same project renders the UI, exposes the API, and enforces auth at the edge. Business logic lives in a clean service layer between the API routes and the database, so rules are enforced in exactly one place.

11 — Trust

Security & reliability

Layered access control

Role checks run at the network edge (middleware) and again in every server handler — defence in depth. Technicians can only ever touch their own jobs.

Hardened auth

Passwords are bcrypt-hashed; sessions use short-lived access tokens with rotating refresh tokens; login is rate-limited to slow brute-force attempts.

Verified workforce

No self-registered account can sign in until an admin approves it — the workforce list is always vetted.

Direct-to-cloud uploads

Photos upload straight from the phone to S3 via short-lived presigned URLs — the server never proxies the file, and the keys stay server-side.

Full audit trail

Significant actions are written to an immutable audit log; every job keeps a complete status history with actor and timestamp.

Validated everywhere

The same Zod schema validates input on the client and re-validates it on the server, so bad data can't slip through.

12 — Operations

Deployment & running it

Cleanify is built to deploy as a standalone Next.js app on a modern hosting platform (e.g. Vercel), backed by managed MongoDB Atlas and an AWS S3 bucket. Configuration is entirely by environment variables — nothing sensitive is baked into the code.

Hosting

Standalone Next.js build; one deploy serves web console, API and field app.

Managed data

MongoDB Atlas for the database, AWS S3 for photo storage — both scale without ops overhead.

Quality gates

Strict TypeScript, linting and an end-to-end test harness guard every change.

13 — The pitch

Why it sells

For a prospect, Cleanify is the difference between running a cleaning business on memory and running it on a system. The talking points that land:

  • Proof on every job. Timestamped before/after photos protect the business in disputes and reassure customers.
  • The office always knows. Real-time status from the field means no chasing crews for updates.
  • One system, many services. Water tanks today, any cleaning service tomorrow — without a rebuild.
  • Field-ready. An installable app that works on a phone, on a weak connection, with a guided flow crews can't get wrong.
  • Accountable by design. Verified workforce, role-based access, and a full audit trail — trust built in, not bolted on.
  • Owns its data. Reviews, attendance and productivity reporting turn day-to-day work into numbers the owner can act on.
In one line

Cleanify connects the office and the field for a multi-service cleaning company — bookings in, crews out, proof and ratings back — on one modern, secure, extensible platform.
